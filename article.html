kW vs kWh: the small distinction that explains your energy system

One measures power. The other measures energy. Here is how to tell them apart without getting lost in the abbreviations.

SmartGridTwin editorialUpdated September 2026 · 8 min read

Electricity becomes easier to understand when you separate two ideas: how quickly energy is being used, and how much energy is used over time.

The short versionkW is a rate. kWh is an amount.

A 5 kW appliance can use energy quickly. If it runs for two hours, it uses 10 kWh.

Power is the rate

Power describes how quickly a device is transferring or using energy. It is measured in watts (W), or more commonly kilowatts (kW). A fast EV charger has a higher power rating than a household wall socket because it can deliver energy at a higher rate.

Energy is the total

Energy is power multiplied by time. It is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh), the unit you see on an electricity bill. A 1 kW heater running for three hours consumes 3 kWh.

P = VIE = Pt

Why it matters at home

Solar panels are often described by their power output in kW. Home batteries are described by their stored energy capacity in kWh. A system can therefore have a 6 kW solar array connected to a 10 kWh battery.
